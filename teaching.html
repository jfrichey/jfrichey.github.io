01

Where it started

HCSSiM

My formal teaching career started in 2015, when I served as junior staff at HCSSiM, six years after I had attended as a student. I was young, nervous, and constantly both excited and exhausted. It was a rewarding and formative experience. The same adjectives apply to my summers as a student and as a staffer.

HCSSiM treats mathematics as a living culture rather than a sequence of prerequisites. Students spend six weeks arguing, experimenting, writing, and learning how much more there is to ask.

University teaching

Courses and materials

As a graduate student and postdoc, I have been the lead instructor for more than fifteen undergraduate courses in probability, linear algebra, and multivariable calculus. The materials repository includes problem sets, quizzes, examinations, and solutions from several past courses.

My current course page is Probability Topics at the Budapest University of Technology and Economics: a walk through random walk, percolation, and first-passage percolation, with student presentations of research papers.

03

2016–2020 · informal mathematics

UW Math Circle

From 2016–2020, I led the second year of the UW Math Circle. Lucas van Meter and I ran weekly two- to three-hour sessions with a diverse group of seventh- and eighth-grade students.

We cooked up our own lesson plans in game theory, group theory, probability, and geometry, and experimented constantly with both the material and our teaching methods. The best sessions usually began with a concrete puzzle and ended somewhere nobody had predicted.

Start with a gameThere are 21 stones. Two players alternately remove 1, 2, or 3. What changes if the final stone loses?
End with structureWhich positions are winning? What is the invariant? Can the strategy survive a different move set?
04

Undergraduate research

Mentoring

I mentored multiple groups of undergraduates in mathematical research through WXML at the University of Washington and REX at UBC. In 2014 I gave a talk at UW’s REU, and later mentored a student project in percolation that resulted in a publication.

Undergraduate research works best when the question is real, the experiments are honest, and the student has room to discover that the first plausible conjecture is rarely the final one.
