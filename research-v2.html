01

Symbolic dynamics · Gibbs measures

Shifts of finite type

How much global structure can be forced by one local prohibition? I study random colorings and binary sequences conditioned to avoid finitely many patterns—models where probability, combinatorics, dynamics, and statistical physics meet.

In one dimension, overlap structure controls entropy. We are asking whether similarly simple statistics control letter and pattern densities, and what survives in higher dimensions and on non-amenable graphs.

02

Interacting particles · Critical phenomena

Self-organized criticality

Sandpiles and activated random walks turn simple local rules into large, unpredictable avalanches. Simulations suggest that these systems organize themselves near a critical state without anyone tuning a parameter by hand.

My work asks for a mathematical account of that picture: critical density, sustained activity, avalanche geometry, and the robustness of the limiting state.

03

Random algorithms · Limit laws

Combinatorial stochastic processes

I am drawn to random processes whose rules are elementary but whose long-run behavior is not: Markov chains, renewal systems, fragmentation and coagulation, random arithmetic, and evolving combinatorial objects.

Typical questions ask for a stationary law, a scaling limit, a sharp hitting time, or even a convincing picture of what the process eventually prefers to do.

04

Random matrices · Statistical inference

Detecting hidden geometry

A large graph may have been generated from points in a low-dimensional space—or it may have no geometry at all. When can an observer distinguish the two possibilities from the adjacency matrix alone?

For Wishart matrices and random intersection graphs, geometry disappears at essentially the same critical dimension. This hints at a universality principle extending beyond either model.

05

Random walk · Inverse problems

Finding the source

Suppose a random walk or spreading process has already run, and all that remains is a snapshot of the sites it visited. Can one recover where it began, where it ended, or which locations it favored?

The applied version is rumor or infection tracing on a network. Even for simple random walk on ℤᵈ, the geometry left behind makes source recovery surprisingly difficult.

06

Random walk · Deterministic perturbations

Permuted random walk

What happens if ordinary random-walk steps alternate with a fixed permutation of the vertices? A permutation can scramble local geometry, and one might expect it to slow escape.

On a regular tree, surprisingly, no permutation can slow the walk down. The open question is how far that rigidity extends—to other graphs, other walks, and other notions of speed.

07

Percolation · Enumerative probability

Counting clusters

Color lattice sites independently open or closed and count the connected open pieces. The number of clusters is plainly proportional to area, but identifying the limiting constant is a surprisingly rich problem.

One approach builds a configuration a few columns at a time, recording how boundary connections merge, split, and disappear.

08

Coverage processes · Random sets

Stochastic geometry

Scatter random arcs, hyperplanes, or other sets through a space. How quickly do they cover it? What does the last uncovered region look like? What random shape remains when many sets intersect?

These questions connect coupon collection to geometry and lead naturally to objects such as the Crofton cell, which emerges as a universal limit in several models.
