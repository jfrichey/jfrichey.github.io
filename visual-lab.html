01 · Activated random walk

Activity wakes activity.

A finite box begins with sleeping particles. A small burst at the center starts walking. Active particles wake sleepers they encounter; an isolated active particle may fall asleep; particles that reach the boundary disappear.

Why it works: even without knowing the definition of ARW, the viewer sees the central tension—activity spreads, but sleep and dissipation try to stop it.

02 · Shifts of finite type & patterns

A local rule changes the whole texture.

Every row is sampled from a binary process in which the word 1001 is forbidden. The slider rewards or penalizes the symbol 1. A four-letter prohibition becomes a visible global bias.

Why it works: the rule is understandable in five seconds, while the animation quietly displays the Gibbs/Parry measures that appear in the research.

03 · Random matrices

Watch geometry become noise.

The heat map is a standardized Wishart matrix built from n Gaussian points in dimension d. As the hidden dimension grows, its eigenvalues settle toward the semicircle law associated with GOE.

Why it works: the matrix entries look noisy at every dimension, but the moving spectrum reveals the geometric structure that entrywise inspection misses.

04 · Combinatorial stochastic processes

The weakest candidate changes everyone’s territory.

Random candidates occupy the unit interval. Voters support the nearest candidate; the smallest Voronoi constituency is eliminated; then every constituency is recomputed. The process repeats to six survivors.

Why it works: this is a genuinely unfamiliar stochastic process that still reads as a tiny political drama—an inviting front door to the broader combinatorial-processes collection.

05 · Random walks

Where did the walker start?

The image records occupation times of a planar simple random walk after four million steps. Brightness tells how often a site was visited, but time has been erased. Click the map to place a guess; then reveal the source.

Why it works: it turns an inverse problem into a ten-second challenge. The question arrives before the terminology, which is exactly the order a broad audience needs.
